# How Chroma makes the music · cheat sheet for questions

**One sentence:** Akshat's model reads the photo (place, light, colours, details). We turn that into two feelings,
energy and pleasantness, using music-psychology research. Those feelings, the place and your walking speed then
pick every musical choice. The music is composed live in the browser (Tone.js), not taken from a library of songs.

## The chain

```
photo / camera ──► Akshat's model ──► scene card ──► feelings ──► musical choices ──► sound (Tone.js)
                   place, time,        (JSON)         energy        genre, style,        real piano,
                   colours, details                   pleasantness  tempo, key, chords,  drum kits,
                                                                    beat, instruments    synths
        + your speed (steps / GPS / camera movement)   + your personality (login)
```

## Every parameter: where it comes from

| Musical choice | Decided by | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Genre family** | the **place** from the model | park → classical · street → hip-hop · station → techno · club → house / drum & bass · market → rumba / flamenco · beach → ambient · square → jazz |
| **Style inside the family** (38 in total) | **energy**, and in walk mode your **speed** | club: deep house (still) → house (walking) → drum & bass (running) → hardcore 200+ bpm (sprinting) |
| **Energy** (0–100%) | saturation and contrast of the colours, the place (a club is more eventful than a park), crowds and traffic, night | Valdez & Mehrabian 1994; Axelsson et al. 2010 |
| **Pleasantness** (0–100%) | brightness and warm vs cool colours, the place, golden hour | Valdez & Mehrabian 1994; Palmer et al. 2013 |
| **Major or minor** | pleasantness: pleasant → bright major (lydian, ionian), unpleasant → dark minor (aeolian, phrygian) | Palmer et al. 2013 |
| **Key** (C, D, E…) | the hue of the photo's main colour: 12 hues → 12 keys | a blue sea and a red sunset give different keys |
| **Tempo** | the style's own range, pushed by energy; when walking, your **steps** (half, same, 1.5× or double, whatever fits the style) | 110 steps/min → 110 bpm lo-fi or 165 bpm drum & bass |
| **Chords** | the colours from top (sky) to bottom (ground), or the style's classic progression (e.g. I–IV–V–I for classical) | more colours → richer chords (7ths, 9ths) |
| **Surprise chord** | one planned unexpected chord just before the end, resolved by the home chord | Cheung et al. 2019: surprise after predictability is most pleasing |
| **Beat** | the style's groove, built from rhythm recipes and slightly varied each time; busy parts of the photo add extra hits | 823 different drum grooves measured |
| **Drum kit** | the style | LinnDrum for hip-hop, 808 for house, acoustic kit for jazz, techno kit for techno |
| **Instruments** | the genre family | real grand piano (classical), guitars (market), Rhodes (jazz), synths (techno), pads and bells (beach) |
| **Extras** (max 2) | details in the photo | birds (trees), waves and gulls (sea), crowd murmur (busy), footsteps (paving), season sounds |
| **Room / echo** | indoor or outdoor, how open the place is, near the sea | small dry room indoors, big echo outdoors |
| **Layers** | how long you've been moving | chords → + drums → + melody → + extras; they fall back when you stop |
| **Personality** | the type picked at login (MUSIC model, Rentfrow et al. 2011) | Mellow: −12 bpm, softer; Intense: +12 bpm, punchier, more minor; Sophisticated: richer chords |

**What never changes the genre:** the light. A park stays classical at night or at noon; only the key, chords and
mood follow the light. The place picks the family, the feeling and speed pick the style.

## Likely questions, short answers

- **Is it AI-generated music?** The *reading* of the place is AI (Akshat's model). The music is generated by rules
  based on music-psychology research, so every choice can be explained. No song is copied.
- **Is every piece different?** Yes. Two photos of the same place get the same genre family but a different key,
  chords, groove, tempo and sound. Measured on 1,000 photos: 823 grooves, about 100 tempos, 38 styles.
- **Why rules and not a neural network for the music?** Explainable (every choice has a reason and a source),
  real time on a phone, and no training data with copyright problems. A music model (e.g. Magenta) is a next step.
- **When does it go to 180–200+ bpm?** Only when you move fast: running in a club gives drum & bass (~170),
  a sprint or bike gives hardcore (200–230). A still photo never goes to the most extreme style.
- **What if the model is unsure?** Below 50% it waits for a second look before changing genre, so one wrong guess
  doesn't switch the music.
- **What if there's no internet / the model sleeps?** The phone reads the colours itself and keeps the last place;
  the music never stops.
- **How fast does it react while walking?** A new view every 3.5 s; the music changes on the next bar, with a
  short transition (filter sweep, snare roll, crash) when the genre changes.
- **Can I keep the music?** Every photo piece can be saved as a WAV file.

## Sources (from Akshat's research note)

Russell 1980 (two-axis model of feeling) · Valdez & Mehrabian 1994 (colour → feeling) · Palmer et al. 2013 PNAS
(colour ↔ music through emotion) · Axelsson, Nilsson & Berglund 2010 (soundscapes: pleasant × eventful) ·
Gabrielsson & Lindström 2010 (emotion → tempo, mode, loudness) · Cheung et al. 2019 (surprise and musical pleasure) ·
Rentfrow, Goldberg & Levitin 2011 (MUSIC model of music taste).
